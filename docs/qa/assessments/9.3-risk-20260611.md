# Risk Profile: 9.3 — 历史日志下载（90 天 + JSON/CSV）

2026-06-11 | Turing (QA) | Comprehensive (security-sensitive) | Companion to `9.3-test-design-20260611.md`

Scoring: **Risk = Probability (1–3) × Impact (1–3)**. ≥6 = High (P0 mitigation mandatory),
3–4 = Medium (P1), 1–2 = Low (P2). This story moves a user's 90-day request history out of
ClickHouse, through OSS, behind a signed URL, to email — every step is a data-exfiltration surface.

## Heat Summary

- **HIGH (≥6): 5** — R-01, R-02, R-03, R-04, R-05. All five are P0-mitigated with multi-level coverage; none may ship unverified.
- **MEDIUM (3–4): 4** — R-06, R-07, R-09 (+R-10 borderline). P1 coverage.
- **LOW (1–2): 3** — R-08, R-10, R-11. P2 coverage.

## Gate Implications

- The 5 HIGH risks ARE the P0 elevation list the Architect mandated. A QA `*review` gate FAILs if any P0 scenario lacks passing evidence.
- **R-05 is the single must-do-before-merge** the Architect flagged HIGH: the EXTEND migration is safe ONLY if the 3 existing 2.6 repo call-sites are kind-scoped. INT-003 is the regression guard; treat a missing kind-scope as a release blocker, not a concern.
- R-04 (CSV injection) and R-02 (PII/cost) are grep-auditable by design — QA review should statically grep the dumper's SQL projection + the CSV writer's escape function as cheap high-signal verification before running tests.

## Residual Risk (accepted)

- E2E executed as `test.skip` stubs (no local integrated stack, [[project_toolchain_env_limits]]). Accepted because every HIGH risk is independently covered at UNIT+INT; the E2E stubs are the CI-job backstop, not the sole guard.
